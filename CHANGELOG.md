## 2026-11-20: Course export (owner request, fourth of five sub-items)
- **Whole course in one file:** in Home → By course, each course heading (not "No course") has an **Export** button that opens the export panel on the whole course. In a lesson that shares its course with other lessons, the export panel has a **This lesson / Whole course · N lessons** choice (starts on This lesson; the extras tick-box is kept when switching). PDF, Word and plain text all work; the file is named "<course> - all lessons".
- **Layout:** course title, a line with lessons, notes and date range, and a numbered list of lessons; then each lesson, oldest first, starts on a new page (PDF) or with a page break (Word, Heading 1) with its date and video time. Notes keep their sections, typed tables, ★ test highlights and times. A lesson's own summary, brain dump, Claude's summary, practice questions and rewatch list come under it as small headings (Word Heading 3), and can be left out with the tick-box. One **Key terms for the whole course** table at the end, each term once, with the lesson it came from. Plain text nests lessons under the course (`#` course, `##` lesson, `###` section).
- Lessons are matched by course name (ignoring spaces at the ends), the same grouping as By course. Nothing new is saved: no lesson field changes and nothing to migrate.
- Tests: 300 browser checks (was 268): lesson order, title and contents, numbered lesson headings, sections and tables kept, the course-wide Key terms table without repeats, extras on and off, plain text nesting, Export buttons on course headings only, the panel from home and from a lesson (switching both ways, tick-box kept, starting on This lesson again), valid PDF (one page per lesson) and well-formed Word file (a page break per lesson, Heading 3 style), no choice for single or course-less lessons, no saved lesson changed, no sideways scrolling in light and dark.
- Why: the owner asked for it for exam revision. Reviewing your own notes helps more than only taking them (Kiewra et al. 1991, see 2026-11-06). Going back over earlier material before a cumulative test is linked to better long-term retention: Khanna, Badura Brack & Finken 2013 (Teaching of Psychology 40(3), 175–182) found psychology classes with cumulative finals did better on content tests given up to 18 months later. Evidence is mixed: Bailey et al. 2021 (PLoS ONE 16(4): e0250143; two biology sections, not randomly assigned) found cumulative midterms made no difference to final-exam scores or overall retention five months later, helping only students with weaker reasoning skills. So the app offers the course file without claims beyond "it helps you go back over everything together".
- Research this week: Kane et al. 2017 (Journal of Experimental Psychology: General 146(11), 1649–1674; N = 182, video lecture) found note-taking did not lower mind-wandering for the group as a whole, but did for students with less prior knowledge and for those who took more and better notes; mind-wandering predicted lower test scores. This supports making note-taking easy rather than adding more on-screen prompts; no new backlog item.
- Sources: blogs.mtroyal.ca/isotl/2013/08/06/short-and-long-term-effects-of-cumulative-finals-on-student-learning/ (summary of Khanna et al. 2013, with page quotes); pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8062001 (Bailey et al. 2021); eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/122001/ (Kane et al. 2017).

- Tests: 246 browser checks (was 227): quiet-stretch rules (start, between notes, to the end, exactly 3 minutes counts, just under doesn't), sorting by time, counts, no map without the clock or notes, the card's summary line, markers on each lane at the right position, shading, the list wording, jump to Organize, the no-clock message, the example lesson having no quiet stretches, no sideways scrolling and visible markers in dark mode.
- Why: attention to a lecture video and memory for it drop as the video goes on. Farley, Risko & Kingstone 2013 (Frontiers in Psychology 4:619; 21 students, 40-minute video lecture) found attention ratings and retention fell with time on task and retention was lower for the second half; they cite Risko et al. 2012 (Applied Cognitive Psychology 26, 234–242) finding more mind-wandering and less memory late in lectures. Gaps in notes as a sign of drifting off is our inference, not something these studies tested directly, so the card says "may have drifted off". Video note apps such as ReClipped and Vidnotation also show notes as marks on the video timeline.
- Research this week: Chen et al. 2024 also found that watching at 2x lowered immediate test scores by about 6 points across both experiments (significant in one), and that taking notes at 2x did about as well as not taking notes at 1x. Added to the "Before you press play" backlog item as an optional speed tip.
- Sources: frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00619/full (Farley et al. 2013, citing Risko et al. 2012); doi.org/10.1002/acp.4166 (Chen et al. 2024); capterra.com/p/268084/ReClipped and apps.apple.com/app/vidnotation/id6532588668 (similar apps).

## 2026-11-06: Tables typed straight into notes (owner request, first of five sub-items)
- **Typing a table:** any note with `|` between at least two filled-in columns is a table row, e.g. `Stage | Where | ATP`. Outer pipes (`| a | b |`) and Markdown divider lines (`---|---`) are fine; a divider shows as a dashed line on screen and is dropped from exports. Empty cells are allowed (`Total | | 38`). A note with several `|` lines (written in Edit) is a small table on its own. Section headings are never tables. Take notes' shortcut line now says `a | b` table row.
- **On screen (Take notes and Organize):** each row note shows its cells in columns. Rows next to each other (same indent) share one set of column widths, sized to the text in each column with room for the longest word, so they line up as one table and words don't break on a 390 px phone (`tableLayout()`). The first row of a run is the header (bold, shaded). A ★ test row is shaded yellow and keeps its ★ Test label. Every row is still its own note, so tapping, selecting, moving, indenting, editing, undo and search highlights work as before.
- **Exports:** consecutive row notes become one real table (`tableRun()`, export block `grid`): PDF draws ruled cells with column widths from the text, a shaded header row that repeats after a page break, and the video time in the margin; Word gets a real table (fixed layout, header row repeats across pages) indented like the notes; Markdown/plain text writes a GitHub-style table. A ★ test row starts with ★ (* in the PDF). Up to 8 columns; extra cells are joined into the last.
- Saved lessons are unchanged: tables are read from the note text, so no new lesson field and nothing to migrate. Old notes that already contain ` | ` with two filled-in sides will now show as a table row.
- Tests: 227 browser checks (was 208): reading rows (outer pipes, dividers, empty cells, no false table from one side), multi-line notes, joining runs (divider dropped, ★ kept, a new indent starts a new table, headings never tables), Markdown table, cells on screen, columns lining up, header row, multi-line table, selecting a row, typing a row in Take notes, no sideways scrolling in light and dark, PDF cells (valid object index) and Word tables (well-formed XML, three columns).
- Why: the owner asked for it. Comparison ("matrix") notes help learners see how ideas relate: Kiewra et al. 1988 (Journal of Educational Psychology 80(4), 595–597) found outline and matrix notes beat linear text notes on recall, and only matrix notes beat them on transfer; Kiewra et al. 1991 (JEP 83(2), 240–245) found matrix notes gave more recall than conventional notes, and that reviewing your own notes beats only taking them. These are older, small lab studies on provided or structured notes, so the benefit for self-typed tables during a video is likely but not proven.
- Research this week (ideas added to the backlog): Wong & Lim 2023 (Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied 29(1), 124–135; two experiments, N = 200, video lectures): longhand note-takers recalled more than learners who photographed slides or took no notes, partly because they mind-wandered less, and learners didn't realise it. Stothart, Mitchum & Yehnert 2015 (JEP: Human Perception and Performance) found that just receiving a phone notification disrupts attention on a task. A 2024 study, "Note-taking fosters distance video learning: smartphones as risk and intellectual values as protective factors" (PMC11266711), points the same way; full text couldn't be read this run.

## 2026-10-30: Brain dump and section cards before the test; weekly update setup
- **Brain dump (Quiz me):** a "Before your test: brain dump" card. Start opens a 2-minute writing box (soft countdown with a chime at 0; flashcards and notes stay hidden while writing). "Check against my notes" saves the text with the lesson (`dump`, `dumpAt`) and shows it next to the lesson's notes, section by section, each with "✓ I had this" / "✗ Missed" (`dumpMarks`, tap again to clear). "Quiz me on the N I missed" runs a quiz of just those sections' recall cards. The card remembers the last check ("Last one Oct 30, 2 of 3 remembered", "See last check"). Switching tabs mid-dump keeps the text. Export includes the brain dump.
- **Section recall cards:** every section with notes becomes a card, "What do you remember about “Section”?", with the section's notes as the answer (`sectionGroups()`; rewatch markers left out). Lessons with sections always have something to practise now, even without Claude.
- **What's new:** a sparkle button in the header (home and lesson screens) with a dot for unseen updates; the panel lists `CHANGES` newest first with "New" on unseen entries; remembered per device (`scribble.news.seen`).
- Home screen: the routine's last step now points to the brain dump.
- **Weekly update setup:** the app moved to `src/app.html`; `scripts/build.py` builds `index.html` (GitHub Pages) and `dist/artifact.html` (claude.ai). `tests/run.sh` (build, staleness, syntax, 61-check browser walk-through in light and dark), GitHub workflows for tests on every push and a daily live-site check, `ROADMAP.md` with binding rules (including the owner's 75% weekly-usage pause) and a researched backlog.
- Why this improvement: retrieval practice (testing yourself) beats rereading by a wide margin on delayed tests (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Dunlosky et al. 2013 rated practice testing "high utility"), and free recall ("brain dump") then checking against notes is a standard way to do it. The owner's test comes right after each video, so recall of what was just learned is what counts. Evidence for tests *during* a lecture is mixed: Szpunar, Khan & Schacter 2013 (PNAS) found interpolated tests cut mind-wandering (19% vs 39–41% of probes) and raised final scores (90% vs 68–76%), but Welhaf et al. 2022 (Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications, 195 students) found only a small drop in mind-wandering and no learning gain, and Boustani & Shanks 2022 (Collabra: Psychology) found the pooled "test-potentiated new learning" effect (g = 0.44) falls to about zero after correcting for publication bias. So this run built the well-supported part (recall after the lesson) and left in-lecture recall as an optional idea in the backlog.
- Sources: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699 (Szpunar et al. 2013); profiles.wustl.edu (Welhaf et al. 2022); discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10144434 (Boustani & Shanks 2022); retrievalpractice.org/braindump; ei.study/edu-praxis and web.colby.edu/cogblog (retrieval practice and Dunlosky et al. 2013 summaries); casrai.org/study-skills/cornell-notes (cover-and-recite step).
