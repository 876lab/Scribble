# Changelog

## 2026-10-30: Brain dump and section cards before the test; weekly update setup
- **Brain dump (Quiz me):** a "Before your test: brain dump" card. Start opens a 2-minute writing box (soft countdown with a chime at 0; flashcards and notes stay hidden while writing). "Check against my notes" saves the text with the lesson (`dump`, `dumpAt`) and shows it next to the lesson's notes, section by section, each with "✓ I had this" / "✗ Missed" (`dumpMarks`, tap again to clear). "Quiz me on the N I missed" runs a quiz of just those sections' recall cards. The card remembers the last check ("Last one Oct 30, 2 of 3 remembered", "See last check"). Switching tabs mid-dump keeps the text. Export includes the brain dump.
- **Section recall cards:** every section with notes becomes a card, "What do you remember about “Section”?", with the section's notes as the answer (`sectionGroups()`; rewatch markers left out). Lessons with sections always have something to practise now, even without Claude.
- **What's new:** a sparkle button in the header (home and lesson screens) with a dot for unseen updates; the panel lists `CHANGES` newest first with "New" on unseen entries; remembered per device (`scribble.news.seen`).
- Home screen: the routine's last step now points to the brain dump.
- **Weekly update setup:** the app moved to `src/app.html`; `scripts/build.py` builds `index.html` (GitHub Pages) and `dist/artifact.html` (claude.ai). `tests/run.sh` (build, staleness, syntax, 61-check browser walk-through in light and dark), GitHub workflows for tests on every push and a daily live-site check, `ROADMAP.md` with binding rules (including the owner's 75% weekly-usage pause) and a researched backlog.
- Why this improvement: retrieval practice (testing yourself) beats rereading by a wide margin on delayed tests (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Dunlosky et al. 2013 rated practice testing "high utility"), and free recall ("brain dump") then checking against notes is a standard way to do it. The owner's test comes right after each video, so recall of what was just learned is what counts. Evidence for tests *during* a lecture is mixed: Szpunar, Khan & Schacter 2013 (PNAS) found interpolated tests cut mind-wandering (19% vs 39–41% of probes) and raised final scores (90% vs 68–76%), but Welhaf et al. 2022 (Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications, 195 students) found only a small drop in mind-wandering and no learning gain, and Boustani & Shanks 2022 (Collabra: Psychology) found the pooled "test-potentiated new learning" effect (g = 0.44) falls to about zero after correcting for publication bias. So this run built the well-supported part (recall after the lesson) and left in-lecture recall as an optional idea in the backlog.
- Sources: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699 (Szpunar et al. 2013); profiles.wustl.edu (Welhaf et al. 2022); discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10144434 (Boustani & Shanks 2022); retrievalpractice.org/braindump; ei.study/edu-praxis and web.colby.edu/cogblog (retrieval practice and Dunlosky et al. 2013 summaries); casrai.org/study-skills/cornell-notes (cover-and-recite step).
