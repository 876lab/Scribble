## 2026-11-05 (owner request): Back up and restore notes
- The owner wants to remove an old Home Screen shortcut without losing notes. On iPad and iPhone a Home Screen web app keeps its own storage, and removing the icon deletes it; Safari can also clear website data. So the home screen now has a **Back up your notes** card (above How Scribble works).
- **Save backup file** writes every lesson to `scribble-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.json` (`makeBackup()`: `{app: "Scribble", kind: "backup", version: 1, exportedAt, lessons, settings}`). On phones and tablets it opens the share sheet (Save to Files, AirDrop, email, WhatsApp); on computers it downloads; in the claude.ai version it uses the downloads capability. "Other ways" → **Copy backup as text** for pasting into a note or email.
- The card shows "Last backup: today / N days ago (N lessons)" and "notes changed since", stored per device (`scribble.lastBackup`); orange when there is no backup or notes changed since a backup older than a day.
- **Restore from a backup** (also offered on an empty home screen as "Have a backup?"): choose the file or paste the text. A preview lists the lessons and says what will happen. Restoring merges (`planRestore()`): adds lessons that aren't here, replaces lessons that are newer in the backup, keeps lessons that are newer here, and never deletes anything. **Undo restore** puts things back as they were (a copy is also kept in `scribble.v1.beforeRestore`). A wrong file gets a plain message.
- Tests: 194 browser checks (was 177): backup file name and contents, last-backup line, losing all data then restoring exactly, preview wording, undo restore, merge rules (newer here kept, missing added, nothing deleted), wrong file, pasted backup.

## 2026-11-02 (owner request): Select several notes, undo deletes, add notes in between
- **Select several (Organize):** a "Select several" button (or Select on a note's toolbar) turns on tick boxes. The toolbar shows "N notes selected", note-type chips that retype all of them, Up, Down, Outdent, Indent and Delete N, plus Clear and Done. Moving works like a block: selected notes move one step together, notes already at the edge stay put, and Up/Down switch off when nothing can move (`moveMany()`, `canMoveMany()`). Moving is off while a search or filter is on, as before. The page keeps its scroll position after each action.
- **Undo deletes:** every delete (one note, several, or emptying a note in Edit) is kept for undo, up to 30 in a row, until the app is closed (`deleteNotes()`, `undoDelete()`). The message ("3 notes deleted · Undo") now stays 8 seconds and sits above the toolbar; an "↶ Undo delete (N)" button stays in Organize after the message goes. Undo puts notes back exactly where they were, newest delete first.
- **Add notes in between (Take notes):** tap any note to add new notes right below it. A banner shows "Adding below: …" with "Add at the end instead". New notes take the chosen note's video time and indent (a point under a section heading); each new note becomes the next insert point, so a run of notes stays in order. "Missed that" also goes in at that point. Tapping the same note again, or switching tabs, turns it off.
- The deleted/restored message is readable in dark mode (underlined Undo instead of yellow on light).
- Tests: 177 browser checks (was 137).

## 2026-10-30: Brain dump and section cards before the test; weekly update setup
- **Brain dump (Quiz me):** a "Before your test: brain dump" card. Start opens a 2-minute writing box (soft countdown with a chime at 0; flashcards and notes stay hidden while writing). "Check against my notes" saves the text with the lesson (`dump`, `dumpAt`) and shows it next to the lesson's notes, section by section, each with "✓ I had this" / "✗ Missed" (`dumpMarks`, tap again to clear). "Quiz me on the N I missed" runs a quiz of just those sections' recall cards. The card remembers the last check ("Last one Oct 30, 2 of 3 remembered", "See last check"). Switching tabs mid-dump keeps the text. Export includes the brain dump.
- **Section recall cards:** every section with notes becomes a card, "What do you remember about “Section”?", with the section's notes as the answer (`sectionGroups()`; rewatch markers left out). Lessons with sections always have something to practise now, even without Claude.
- **What's new:** a sparkle button in the header (home and lesson screens) with a dot for unseen updates; the panel lists `CHANGES` newest first with "New" on unseen entries; remembered per device (`scribble.news.seen`).
- Home screen: the routine's last step now points to the brain dump.
- **Weekly update setup:** the app moved to `src/app.html`; `scripts/build.py` builds `index.html` (GitHub Pages) and `dist/artifact.html` (claude.ai). `tests/run.sh` (build, staleness, syntax, 61-check browser walk-through in light and dark), GitHub workflows for tests on every push and a daily live-site check, `ROADMAP.md` with binding rules (including the owner's 75% weekly-usage pause) and a researched backlog.
- Why this improvement: retrieval practice (testing yourself) beats rereading by a wide margin on delayed tests (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Dunlosky et al. 2013 rated practice testing "high utility"), and free recall ("brain dump") then checking against notes is a standard way to do it. The owner's test comes right after each video, so recall of what was just learned is what counts. Evidence for tests *during* a lecture is mixed: Szpunar, Khan & Schacter 2013 (PNAS) found interpolated tests cut mind-wandering (19% vs 39–41% of probes) and raised final scores (90% vs 68–76%), but Welhaf et al. 2022 (Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications, 195 students) found only a small drop in mind-wandering and no learning gain, and Boustani & Shanks 2022 (Collabra: Psychology) found the pooled "test-potentiated new learning" effect (g = 0.44) falls to about zero after correcting for publication bias. So this run built the well-supported part (recall after the lesson) and left in-lecture recall as an optional idea in the backlog.
- Sources: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699 (Szpunar et al. 2013); profiles.wustl.edu (Welhaf et al. 2022); discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10144434 (Boustani & Shanks 2022); retrievalpractice.org/braindump; ei.study/edu-praxis and web.colby.edu/cogblog (retrieval practice and Dunlosky et al. 2013 summaries); casrai.org/study-skills/cornell-notes (cover-and-recite step).
